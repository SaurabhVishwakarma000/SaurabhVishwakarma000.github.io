My journey

Two years, no shortcuts.

Every developer starts at zero, and I did too — the first few months were mostly broken code and quiet frustration. What changed things was simply not stopping; practice makes perfect isn't just a saying, it's been my entire method.

Over roughly two years of self-directed, hands-on practice, I taught myself full-stack development from the ground up — HTML and CSS, then Node.js and REST APIs, then real-time systems with Socket.io and WebRTC — learning to work with AI tools the same deliberate way: understanding what they're good for, and where a developer still has to think for themselves.

Some of that practice turned into real, paid freelance work — a few of the sites I built are still live today. Most of it stayed quieter: rebuilding the same feature five different ways until I understood exactly why one way was right. Programming used to feel empty and abstract; it doesn't anymore, and that shift is the real experience behind this portfolio.

Outside of code, I bring that same patience to anything mechanical, a good recipe, or a few lines of shayari.

How I work

Five steps, every build.

01 Discover

Get clear on the problem, the users, and the real constraints before any code gets written.

02 Architect

Plan the data model, the API surface, and how the frontend and backend fit together.

03 Build

Write the backend and frontend in tandem — REST endpoints, then the UI that uses them.

04 Test

Check it against real data and edge cases, across browsers and screen sizes.

05 Deploy

Ship it, watch how it performs in the real world, and keep improving from there.

Founder

24Zen isn't dead. It's paused.

Before I was building products for other people, I tried building one of my own. 24Zen was designed as a single always-on platform — one login, six verticals, help available 24x7 — the kind of one-stop destination Flipkart and Amazon were never built to be. How it's different isn't something I'll spell out here; that's a claim I'd rather prove with a live product than argue in a paragraph.

It had a small team, a complete brand identity, a working local-services build, and a 12-slide investor deck behind it — everything except the capital to scale. Rather than let it limp along underfunded, I made the call to pause it.

Paused is the operative word. 24Zen restarts — not if, when, whatever it takes to get there.

Status

Paused. Not closed.

Relaunching when the capital is right — on my own terms, however long that takes.
